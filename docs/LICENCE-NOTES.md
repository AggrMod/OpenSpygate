# Licence notes for third-party sources

Status: research notes, 2026-11-14, by Ledger (licence researcher, #6). Not legal advice. These notes report what each rights holder's own page says, and what could not be checked.

**Method.** Each source was checked against the rights holder's own licence or terms page, using web fetch. Quotes are the text the fetch tool returned from those pages. They were not compared byte-for-byte with a saved copy, so re-check a quote before relying on it wording-for-wording. Where only a forum post, a third-party page, or a search summary was found, the row says "could not verify". The question asked for each source is whether files from it may be redistributed inside a public repository whose assets are CC BY-SA 4.0. The project's CONTRIBUTING.md already limits accepted licences to CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, MIT, BSD, Apache-2.0 and OFL, and excludes NC and ND.

## Per-source notes

**Mixamo (Adobe): could not verify.** The Mixamo site returned only a "Loading Mixamo" page, and the Mixamo FAQ URL returned 404 or 403. Adobe's general terms page at [adobe.com/legal/terms.html](https://www.adobe.com/legal/terms.html) does not mention Mixamo. It does say: "under no circumstances can you distribute the Content Files on a stand-alone basis, outside of the End Use." Whether that clause applies to Mixamo files was not confirmed. The only Mixamo licence text found is in Adobe community forum posts, which are secondary and were not treated as the rights holder's terms. Those posts say the main restriction is distributing the raw files. CONTRIBUTING.md already excludes Mixamo, and this research does not change that.

**Sonniss GDC bundle: no.** The licence grants "a worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free license to use all or any of the sound effects". It also says: "Licensee may not distribute, publish, sub-license or otherwise supply the sound effects as sound effects to any other person", and "Licensee may not sell any of the sound effects as they come." It prohibits "using any sound effects licensed under this Agreement for the purpose of training". The version found is "version 2.0 of this Agreement, effective 27 August 2026". Earlier bundle years may have different terms, and the research doc's claim that each year has its own PDF was not confirmed on the page (the page links every year to the same licence URL). Sounds from this bundle can be used in the shipped game, but not put in the repo as files.

**Kenney: yes.** The support page says: "Yes, all game assets on the asset pages are public domain licensed (CC0)." It adds: "You're free to use them, even in commercial projects." It also says: "Attribution is not required." It asks users not to use the Kenney logo separately. The page does not separately state a redistribution rule. CC0 is the licence named.

**Quaternius (UAL1 and UAL2): yes.** Both pack pages say "Free to use in personal, educational and commercial projects." with the licence shown as CC0. Neither page states an attribution rule. The pages do not mention redistribution specifically.

**KayKit: yes.** The GitHub repo page says: "Licensed under CC0 1.0 Universal, see LICENSE.txt for more information." Its feature list says: "Free for personal and commercial use, no attribution required. (CC0 Licensed)". The itch.io page was checked and did not show a licence. The Unity Asset Store versions carry a different licence, which was not checked here.

**Poly Haven: yes.** The licence page says: "Our assets are all licensed as CC0, which is effectively Public Domain". It also says: "You can redistribute them, share them around, include them when sharing your own work, or even in a product you sell." It also says "You do not need to give credit or attribution when using them (although it is appreciated)." The API terms in the research doc were not checked in this pass.

**ambientCG: yes.** The licence page says: "All ambientCG assets are provided under the Creative Commons CC0 1.0 Universal License." It says: "You can include the raw files in your project, for example a video game." It adds: "You don't need to give credit but I would of course appreciate it, if you did it anyways."

**Freesound: yes for CC0 and CC BY, per sound, with credit for CC BY; no for CC BY-NC.** The FAQ describes CC0 as "for the 'zero' license you can do pretty much what you want with the sound." For CC BY it says: "For 'attribution' you should always mention the original creators of the sounds when you use them." The FAQ's table says a CC BY sound can be redistributed under CC BY or CC BY-NC, and cannot be redistributed under CC0. For CC BY-NC it says: "'Noncommercial' works like attribution, but you can't earn any money with the piece of work you create!" Sampling+ is legacy; the FAQ says it is being removed, and its redistribution rules were not confirmed. Each Freesound file's licence must be checked at download and logged.

**CMU Motion Capture Database: could not verify.** The official site could not be fetched because the fetch tool reported a certificate error, and a path guess at the CMU FAQ also failed. A search summary said the data is "free for all uses", but this came from a third-party page, not CMU. A KIT-hosted FAQ also gives an acknowledgement line ("Motion data from the CMU Graphics Lab Motion Capture Database was obtained from mocap.cs.cmu.edu."), but KIT is not the rights holder. The FBX conversion on 4TU is listed as CC BY-NC 4.0 by its own host, so a conversion's licence must be checked separately. The research doc's claims that CMU is commercially usable and not resellable are not confirmed here.

**Kokoro-82M: yes with conditions for the model; output could not verify.** The model card says "License: apache-2.0". It says: "Kokoro was trained exclusively on permissive/non-copyrighted audio data and IPA phoneme labels." It also says: "We welcome the deployment of the model in real use cases." The card does not state a licence for the voicepacks (it links to a VOICES.md that was not read), and it does not state who owns or licenses generated audio. So whether generated WAV files can be shipped is not confirmed from this page. The espeak-ng phonemizer is GPLv3 per the research doc. That was not verified on the card, which only mentions it in install commands. Shipping only baked audio and not the GPL code is the approach the research doc describes.

**Google Fonts families: yes with conditions.** The OFL text copies in the Google Fonts GitHub repo name SIL Open Font License 1.1 for Oswald, Bebas Neue (Dharma Type), Barlow Condensed, Inter, Courier Prime, Space Mono, IBM Plex Sans and IBM Plex Mono. IBM Plex carries the reserved font name "Plex". Special Elite is under Apache License 2.0. The Google Fonts licensing page itself returned only its title, so the OFL copies from the repo are the source used here.

**Kevin MacLeod / incompetech: yes with conditions.** The FAQ gives the credit template: "Title Kevin MacLeod (incompetech.com) Licensed under Creative Commons: By Attribution 4.0" with a link to the CC BY 4.0 licence. For video games, the FAQ says "credits are placed on a 'Credits' screen found in the settings menu." It also says: "If you expect your game to be streamed, email me and I'll try to whitelist the title." For uses other than YouTube, the FAQ says "Probably. Be sure to credit me or purchase a license." The earlier research tagged this [U]; this pass confirms CC BY 4.0. The CC BY 4.0 legal code itself was not fetched.

**MakeHuman / MPFB output: yes with conditions.** The MakeHuman LICENSE.md has a section headed "D. Concerning the output from MakeHuman". It says exports "(FBX, OBJ, DAE, MHX2...)" and saved model files are the user's own data. The bundled assets section says the assets are CC0, which means "no restriction on what users can do with the combined output". It adds that third-party assets from asset repositories "are governed by their own licenses, which may differ from the bundled assets' license." The MPFB asset licence file names "Creative Commons CC0 1.0 Universal". The research doc's "outputs are CC0" wording is looser than this: the page supports CC0 for the bundled assets, and says output is the user's data. The makehuman.org and makehumancommunity.org pages returned a parked page or a certificate error, so the repository is the source used.

## Gaps and next steps

1. Mixamo: the official FAQ or terms were not reachable. Keep Mixamo files out of the repo as CONTRIBUTING.md already says.
2. CMU: the official site was not reachable. Get a fetchable CMU licence page, or ask the project lead whether CMU data is acceptable.
3. Kokoro: check VOICES.md for voicepack terms, and check whether the model's maintainers state anything about output.
4. Sonniss: re-check the licence each year the bundle is used.
5. Freesound: log each file's licence at download.
