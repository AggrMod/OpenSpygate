# The Story

Version 0.1, 2026-11-14. A proposal for the project lead. Nothing here is final until he says so.

The story comes first. The narrative (scenes, missions, dialogue) is written from it afterwards.

## 1. The one string

The story follows a single thread of real spycraft that runs unbroken from the year the game begins to the present day: **the illegal and the swap.**

An "illegal" is an intelligence officer sent abroad to live for years, sometimes decades, as someone else: an invented person with an invented past, called a legend. No embassy, no diplomatic protection. If they are caught, the only way home is to be traded for someone the other side wants.

### Why this string

- **It starts where we start.** The 1962 bridge exchange happens in our first city, in our first year.
- **It never stopped.** The same practice surfaces in 1962, 1985, 2010 and 2024.
- **The craft barely changed.** A legend, a dead drop, a signal, a coded message, a swap. The technology around them changed; they did not.
- **It runs through our cities.** Berlin, Vienna, Moscow, and Turkey.
- **It is a story about people.** Living as someone else costs something, and families pay part of it.
- **It is the door.** Stepping into a legend is walking through a door that does not open the other way.

### Still to verify before it is stated as fact in the game

- That legends were often built on the identities of children who had died young.
- The communication methods used by the 2010 group.
- The disputed details of the 1985 and 1986 exchanges.

## 2. Rules for turning it into fiction

- No real person appears, living or dead. No real agency is a playable side. The real events are the skeleton; every name, face and plot is invented.
- Both services have decent people and compromised ones. Neither country is the villain.
- Real families are not re-enacted. The theme of children who did not know is handled through invented characters, with care.
- Real history is told straight in the in-game codex, with sources.

## 3. The story

**Working title: The Legend.**

The story does not follow one person. It follows one legend, a single invented identity, as it is worn, lost, inherited and finally questioned over sixty-five years. The player meets it first as the hunter, then as the handler, then as the one who must wear it.

### Prologue: the door

Black. Nothing. One door, old, paint worn where hands have pushed it, light under it.

A voice, calm, not unkind: *"If you walk in, there is no turning back."*

You have no name yet and no face. You open the door.

### Chapter 1. Berlin, 1961 to 1962: The Bridge

You step through into West Berlin in August 1961, the week the border is sealed. On this side of the door you are given a name, papers and a first job: the lowest member of a counter-intelligence watch team.

The team is watching a quiet man who repairs cameras in a tenement shop. He is polite, dull and liked by his neighbours. He is too clean. Over a winter you learn the trade by learning him: his routes, the chalk mark he never seems to make, the loose brick in a bombed church, the radio he listens to at a set hour.

You catch him. Nobody celebrates. He gives a name that turns out not to be his.

In February you stand in the cold on a bridge at the edge of the city and watch him walk east, traded for someone coming west. Halfway across he looks back, at you, the way a teacher looks at a student who has passed.

In his empty shop you find what he left for whoever came looking: the papers of the life he lived. The name on them belonged to someone else before him.

**What the player learns:** watching, shaking a tail, dead drops, signal marks, the swap.

### Chapter 2. Vienna, 1975: The Paper City

Fourteen years on. You are a handler now, in the neutral city where both sides drink in the same coffeehouses and where legends are made: birth records, school reports, a childhood assembled from paper.

Your service has decided to answer in kind. You build a legend and choose the officer who will wear it. You teach them everything the camera repairer taught you without knowing it.

At the end you stand in a dark hallway beside a door, and you hear yourself say the words: *"If you walk in, there is no turning back."*

You are the voice now. You may have been all along.

**What the player learns:** building a cover, recruiting on trust, cut-outs, working where violence is forbidden.

### Chapter 3. Moscow, 1985: The Year Everything Broke

Your officer has lived the legend for ten years inside the hardest city in the world. You play them: watched constantly, hours of careful walking before every meeting.

Then the networks begin to fall, one after another, too fast to be bad luck. Someone inside your own service is selling names. Your officer's is on the list.

The chapter is one long escape: a signal in a shopping bag, a run to lose the watchers, a ride to the border in the boot of a car, and a nerve held at the checkpoint.

They get out. Others do not. The one who sold them is not found.

**What the player learns:** the full surveillance-detection run, signal sites, exfiltration.

### Chapter 4. Berlin, 1989: The Files

The Wall opens. In the offices of the other side, shredders run day and night and crowds are at the doors.

You are old now and back where you began. Somewhere in those rooms is the file that names the traitor of 1985, and the file of the camera repairer from 1961. You have days before both are pulp or in someone else's hands.

You find the first. The second is already gone, and what is left shows that the legend you found in the shop was not retired when he crossed the bridge. It was given to someone new. It is still in use.

**What the player learns:** the mole hunt, working a city in chaos, choosing what to save.

### Chapter 5. Vienna, 2010: The Tarmac

A new century and a new player character: the grown child of the officer you ran through Moscow, now in the service themselves, raised on half-truths about what their parent did.

Ten people who lived for years as ordinary suburban neighbours are arrested in one morning. One of them carries the old legend, three bearers on from the camera repairer.

You work the exchange: who is traded for whom, and what your side gives up. On a July afternoon two planes park nose to tail at Vienna airport, and people walk between them in single file. You watch the legend go home again.

Your parent, watching the news, says only: *"It is always a bridge."*

**What the player learns:** the same craft with new tools, negotiation, the long game.

### Chapter 6. Ankara, 2024, and now: The Other Side of the Door

The largest exchange since the Cold War. Among those coming home is a couple who wore borrowed names for a decade, with children who are learning on the flight who they are.

You are sent to meet one of those children, now grown, because the service wants to make them an offer: the legend needs a new bearer, and nobody could wear it more naturally.

The last scene is the first scene. The void, the door, the light under it. This time you stand beside it, and they stand in front of it, and the line is yours to say, or not to say.

The player chooses. The game does not tell them which is right.

## 4. What this changes in the design

The design document set each world in its own unrelated year. The story needs the cities in order, moving forward in time:

| Chapter | City | Design document said | Story proposes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Berlin | 1961 to 1963 | 1961 to 1962 (same) |
| 2 | Vienna | 1950s | 1975 |
| 3 | Moscow | 1985 | 1985 (same) |
| 4 | Berlin | none | 1989, the same districts 28 years on |
| 5 | Vienna | none | 2010 |
| 6 | Ankara and Istanbul | Istanbul 1943 | 2024 |

Lisbon 1941, Istanbul 1943 and Havana 1962 stay in the game as **archive cases**: self-contained operations the player opens from the service's archive, each showing where a piece of the craft came from.

This is a decision for the project lead. If he agrees, the design document is updated to match.
