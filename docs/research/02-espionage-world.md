# 02 — The Real World of Espionage (research for *OpenSpygate*)

Status: research pass 1, 2026-11-14. Public historical and journalistic sources only. This is fact-gathering. Fictionalization (agency names, characters, invented operations) happens in a later design pass.

**How to read the confidence tags**
- No tag: the fact appears in at least one solid source cited inline, and other sources agree.
- **[Disputed]:** sources conflict. Both versions are given.
- **[GK]:** general knowledge, widely repeated, but **not verified with a source in this pass**. Check it before it goes into in-game text that presents itself as fact.

Sources are listed inline and gathered in the **Sources** section at the end.

## 0. TL;DR for the design team

- The "spy city" pattern repeats: **a neutral or divided place, at a border, crowded with embassies, exiles and international bodies, where the local police tolerate foreign spying as long as it isn't aimed at them.** Lisbon, Istanbul, Tangier, Vienna and Bern are the purest examples (Cold War Berlin is the divided-city version). That pattern *is* the game's world-building rule.
- The core tradecraft loop is very game-friendly and non-violent: **spot → assess → develop → recruit → handle → (exfiltrate or terminate the relationship)**, run through dead drops, signal sites, brush passes and surveillance-detection routes. Almost none of the documented, famous tradecraft needs combat.
- Recommended vertical slice: **Berlin, "Bridge & Tunnel," set in a stylized early-1960s.** See section 6.

## 1. Countries and their intelligence services

One paragraph each: reputation, signature methods, famous publicly documented operations. Real agency names appear here for research only; see section 5 for how to fictionalize them.

### United States: CIA (foreign intelligence), FBI (counterintelligence), NSA (signals)
**Reputation:** the best-funded and most technology-heavy service. It is famous for reconnaissance technology (U-2, the CORONA satellite) and for gadget-making by its Office of Technical Service. It was also famously penetrated by its own insiders (Aldrich Ames at the CIA, Robert Hanssen at the FBI). **Signature methods:** technical collection; the "Moscow Rules" discipline developed by CIA officers working under heavy KGB surveillance (long surveillance-detection runs on foot and on public transit before any meeting; "if you see it twice over time and distance, you're being watched") ([Moscow Times](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/archive/russian-and-american-spies-square-off); [Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2013/05/17/the-moscow-rules-still-rule/)). **Documented operations:** the Berlin Tunnel / Operation GOLD, a joint operation with Britain, 1955–56 (see Berlin); CORONA, the first successful US photo-reconnaissance satellite, whose first mission in August 1960 photographed more of the USSR than all previous U-2 flights combined ([History.com via search summary](https://www.history.com/articles/pigeon-cameras-and-other-cia-cold-war-spy-gear)); and the FBI's 2001 arrest of Hanssen at a dead drop in Foxstone Park, Virginia ([FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/history/famous-cases/robert-hanssen); [Wikipedia: Foxstone Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foxstone_Park)). Its WWII predecessor, the OSS, ran Allen Dulles's station in Bern ([US National Archives blog](https://text-message.blogs.archives.gov/2012/11/09/allen-dulles-and-no-23-herrengasse-bern-switzerland-1942-1945)).

### United Kingdom: MI6/SIS (foreign), MI5 (security service), GCHQ (signals)
**Reputation:** the oldest modern "secret service" brand, with a mystique heavily shaped by fiction: Ian Fleming was a WWII naval-intelligence officer, and John le Carré served in MI5 and MI6 **[GK]**. Its successes in deception and agent-running are celebrated. It was also badly damaged by the Cambridge Five, the Soviet-recruited moles who included Kim Philby. **Signature methods:** running double agents and deception. MI5's WWII **Double Cross System**, coordinated from January 1941 by the "Twenty Committee" (XX) chaired by J.C. Masterman, "actively ran and controlled the German espionage system in this country" ([MI5: World War II](https://www.mi5.gov.uk/world-war-ii-0); [Wikipedia: Double-Cross System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-Cross_System)). **Documented operations:** Double Cross, including agents GARBO (Juan Pujol García) and TRICYCLE (Dusko Popov), both linked to wartime Lisbon. Operation PIMLICO, the 1985 exfiltration of KGB officer Oleg Gordievsky from Moscow to Finland (see section 3). The Penkovsky case, run jointly with the CIA, 1961–62 (see Moscow). Operation Mincemeat (1943), the deception carried out with a dead body and planted papers **[GK, not verified this pass]**.

### Russia / USSR: Cheka → NKVD → KGB; GRU (military); today SVR (foreign), FSB (domestic)
**Reputation:** the archetypal adversary service, with huge scale, patience and ruthlessness. It is famous for "illegals" (officers placed abroad under false identities, with no diplomatic immunity) and for long-term moles ([Spy Museum glossary](https://spymuseum.org/education-programs/spy-resources/language-of-espionage/)). **Signature methods:** recruiting ideological believers and paying mercenaries (Philby and the Cambridge Five; later Ames and Hanssen, who were paid); saturation surveillance at home, which is why the CIA had to write the "Moscow Rules"; and technical bugging. **Documented operations:** "The Thing," the Great Seal bug. Soviet schoolchildren (Pioneers) presented a carved wooden Great Seal to US Ambassador Averell Harriman on 4 Aug 1945. It hid a passive, battery-free resonant-cavity microphone designed by Léon Theremin. It was found in 1952 **[Disputed: some say 1951]** ([Crypto Museum](https://cryptomuseum.com/covert/bugs/thing); [Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/2015/12/08/theremins-bug)). The KGB learned of the Berlin Tunnel from George Blake before digging began, and let it run to protect him ([Encyclopedia.com: Berlin Tunnel](https://www.encyclopedia.com/politics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/berlin-tunnel); Vogel, *Betrayal in Berlin*, [Hachette](https://www.hachette.co.uk/titles/steve-vogel/betrayal-in-berlin/9781473674042/)). Rudolf Abel was a KGB illegal in New York who was swapped on the Glienicke Bridge in 1962.

### East Germany: Stasi (MfS) and its foreign arm, the HVA
**Reputation:** two faces. At home, the Stasi was one of history's most pervasive surveillance states. Its Berlin headquarters at Normannenstraße is now a museum and memorial, preserving Minister Erich Mielke's office ([visitBerlin](https://www.visitberlin.de/en/stasi-museum); [museumsportal-berlin](https://www.museumsportal-berlin.de/en/museums/stasimuseum/)). Abroad, the HVA under Markus Wolf, "the man without a face," is widely judged one of the most effective Cold War foreign services ([Persuasion](https://www.persuasion.community/p/cold-war-spies-and-authoritarian-lies); [HNN](https://www.historynewsnetwork.org/article/31698)). **Signature methods:** concealed cameras of every kind (in buttonholes, books, plant pots, petrol cans, and the doors of Trabant cars) ([Imaging Resource](https://imaging-resource.com/news/2013/09/19/a-look-at-the-incredible-hidden-cameras-of-the-stasi)); long-term placement of agents inside West German institutions; and "Romeo" agents who seduced secretaries in government offices (**not for game use**, see section 5). **Documented operations:** the **Guillaume affair**. Günter Guillaume, an HVA agent, became an aide to West German Chancellor Willy Brandt. His exposure triggered Brandt's resignation in May 1974. Guillaume was traded back to the East in 1981 ([BStU publication](https://www.bstu.bund.de/assets/bstu/de/Publikationen/BFi32_Muenkel_Stasi_und_Brandt.pdf); [Wikipedia: Guillaume affair](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Guillaume_affair)). Historians argue over whether the affair was a *trigger* or the real *cause* of the resignation **[Disputed]**.

### West Germany: BND (and BfV, domestic)
**Reputation:** the BND was founded on the US-sponsored Gehlen Organization, which had ex-Wehrmacht roots, and was heavily penetrated by East German and Soviet agents **[GK]**. In this game it is the "home team" whose files the HVA kept reading. **Documented:** one account says West German services deciphered old HVA radio congratulation messages, which helped expose Guillaume ([Wikipedia: Guillaume affair](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Guillaume_affair)) **[detail weakly sourced]**. Game value: the BND/HVA rivalry gives German-on-German "same language, same streets, different side" missions.

### Israel: Mossad (foreign), Shin Bet (domestic), Aman (military)
**Reputation:** small, audacious and operationally daring. Its reputation was built on long-range operations far from home. **Signature methods:** small teams, forged documents, safe houses and elaborate cover stories. **Documented operation:** the 1960 capture of Adolf Eichmann in Buenos Aires, directed personally by Mossad chief Isser Harel with Ben-Gurion's approval. Agents entered under false identities. Eichmann was seized on 11 May 1960, held in a safe house for about 8–9 days, then flown out disguised as airline crew and sedated, using the cover of an official Israeli delegation to Argentina's 150th-anniversary celebrations ([Jewish Virtual Library](https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/jsource/Holocaust/eichcap.html); [Mémorial de la Shoah](https://juger-eichmann.memorialdelashoah.org/exhibition/capture.html); [Andrew Nagorski](https://andrewnagorski.com/articles/how-they-captured-adolf-eichmann)). **Game note:** this operation is tied to the Holocaust and belongs in history and lore, **not** in a playable mission (section 5).

### China: MSS (Ministry of State Security), plus military intelligence
**Reputation:** patient, long-horizon recruitment, often through ethnic, family or professional ties, with a strong emphasis on technology and economic secrets. **Documented operation:** **Larry Wu-tai Chin**, a CIA translator and analyst. He was recruited in the 1940s by Communist intelligence (then the Central Investigation Department, the precursor of the MSS) and spied for about 30+ years, reportedly earning more than US$1 million. He was exposed in 1985 by a Chinese defector, Yu Qiangsheng, convicted in February 1986, and died in his cell before sentencing ([Wikipedia: Larry Wu-tai Chin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Wu-tai_Chin); [PBS Frontline](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/pages/frontline/shows/spy/spies/four.html)). The MSS in its present form dates from 1983 **[GK, not verified this pass]**.

### France: DGSE (foreign, formerly SDECE), DST/DGSI (domestic)
**Reputation:** independent-minded, and willing to act alone or against allies' interests. Strong in Africa and the Middle East **[GK]**. **Documented operation:** the **Farewell Dossier**. KGB Colonel Vladimir Vetrov ("Farewell") passed about **4,000 documents** on Soviet technology theft (Line X) in 1981–82. He was handled by France's **domestic** counterintelligence service, the **DST**, not the DGSE. President Mitterrand told Reagan about the source privately at the July 1981 Ottawa G7 summit and shared the material. According to the CIA's own study it led to a "potent counterintelligence response" across NATO ([CIA Studies in Intelligence, "The Farewell Dossier"](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/studies-in-intelligence/1996-2/the-farewell-dossier); [Wikipedia: Farewell Dossier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farewell_Dossier)). The CIA article says only "French intelligence"; the DST attribution comes from Wikipedia and French accounts. Vetrov was later executed in the USSR after an unrelated crime **[GK]**. The 1985 *Rainbow Warrior* sinking by DGSE agents (a photographer was killed) is a real-world scandal that should **not** be used as content.

### Cuba: DI (Dirección de Inteligencia)
**Reputation:** routinely described as "punching above its weight", a small country's service with an outsized record of penetrating the US ([National Interest](https://nationalinterest.org/node/18410); [E-IR](https://www.e-ir.info/2024/05/31/cuban-intelligence-after-the-cold-war-a-case-study-in-adaptation-and-influence/)). **Signature methods:** recruiting idealists early and patiently. Ana Montes was recruited before she joined the US Defense Intelligence Agency, and she received her instructions by shortwave numbers broadcasts and one-time pads **[GK for the radio detail]**. The service also watched exile communities in Miami. **Documented:** **Ana Montes**, a senior DIA Cuba analyst, spied for about 16 years (arrested 2001). The **Wasp Network** (*Red Avispa*) was broken up by the FBI in 1998. Its record of obtaining classified material was "mixed", and its main effort went into watching exile groups ([Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2014/12/17/the_mysterious_cuban_spy_at_the_center_of_obamas_havana_rapprochement/); [National Interest](https://nationalinterest.org/node/18410)). Membership counts conflict **[Disputed]**. Several sources on Cuba are partisan, so they are weighted accordingly.

### Others worth a world or a faction later
- **Switzerland (neutral host):** not a spy power. Its significance is as a stage: Bern and Geneva in WWII (see section 2).
- **Austria (neutral host):** Vienna (see section 2).
- **Portugal and Spain (neutral hosts, WWII):** Lisbon and Madrid. Portugal's secret police (PVDE) largely left foreign spies alone unless internal politics were involved ([search summary of Lisbon sources](https://www.thejamesbonddossier.com/content/james-bond-where-it-all-began.htm)).
- **Turkey (neutral until Feb 1945):** Istanbul and Ankara.
- **Czechoslovakia (StB):** produced Karl Koecher, a mole inside the CIA who was swapped on the Glienicke Bridge in 1986 ([Expats.cz](https://expats.cz/czech-news/article/how-a-czech-mole-infiltrated-the-cia-and-walked-across-the-bridge-of-spies)).

## 2. The classic spy cities

The pattern again: **neutrality + border or chokepoint + embassies and international organizations + a tolerant or bribable local police + transient crowds (refugees, traders, tourists).** Each entry gives why it became a spy city, its documented landmarks, and a playable-district pitch.

### Berlin (Cold War, 1945–1989)
**Why:** a divided city 160 km inside East Germany, where all four occupying powers met face to face. Until the Wall went up in 1961 it had an open sector border, and afterwards it was the hardest border on earth. The Western and Soviet blocs sat at their closest point here **[GK]**.
**Documented landmarks:**
- **Glienicke Bridge, the "Bridge of Spies"** (Berlin–Potsdam). On **10 Feb 1962** KGB illegal Rudolf Abel was exchanged for U-2 pilot Francis Gary Powers, with US student Frederic Pryor released at Checkpoint Charlie at the same time. East German lawyer Wolfgang Vogel brokered it; he later brokered many more swaps. On **11 Feb 1986** Soviet dissident Anatoly Shcharansky and Western agents were exchanged for Karl Koecher and other Eastern agents **[Disputed: head counts vary by source]** ([CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/news/world/russia-west-prisoner-swap-history-1.7282706); [NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/id/wbna38155284); [Moscow Times](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/archive/spy-swaps-examples-from-the-past)).
- **The Berlin Tunnel (Operation GOLD/STOPWATCH),** a CIA–MI6 tunnel of roughly 450 m **[Disputed: 300–450 m]** from Rudow in the American sector to Alt-Glienicke in the East. It tapped Soviet phone cables from May 1955 until Soviet and East German troops "discovered" it on **21–22 April 1956**. George Blake had betrayed it to the KGB from the planning stage; he was the minute-taker at the planning meeting. It was modelled on an earlier British tunnel in Vienna, Operation SILVER. A section is displayed at the Allied Museum ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/politics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/berlin-tunnel); [visitBerlin event page](https://www.visitberlin.de/en/event/final-secrets-operation-gold-berlin-spy-tunnel); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/world%2Foceania%2Fcold-war-spy-tunnel-under-berlin-found-in-forest-after-56-years-1.1063975)).
- **Stasi HQ, Normannenstraße (Lichtenberg),** now the Stasi Museum, with Mielke's preserved office and displays of concealed cameras ([visitBerlin](https://www.visitberlin.de/en/stasi-museum)).
- **Checkpoint Charlie** (Allied crossing point on Friedrichstraße; US–Soviet tank standoff in Oct 1961) and **Teufelsberg** (US/UK listening station on a hill of wartime rubble) **[both GK, not verified this pass]**.
**Playable district pitch:** *Look:* rain-slicked cobbles, sodium lamps, bombed-out gaps between buildings, the Wall as a hard level boundary with watchtowers and searchlights, a lakeside bridge at dawn. *Mood:* tense, grey, procedural. *Era:* stylized 1961–63. *Signature missions:* prisoner exchange on the bridge (escort, timing, sightlines); tunnel tap (dig, patch the cable, then hold out as the opposing team closes in); sector crossing with a forged pass; spot the tail on the U-Bahn; recover film from a dead drop in a bombed church. The two-sided city gives a natural **two-team PvP split**.

### Vienna (post-1945 occupation; neutral after 1955; still active)
**Why:** occupied by the four powers in 1945–55, like Berlin. It declared neutrality in 1955 and became a seat of international organizations (the Vienna International Centre opened in 1979, with the IAEA, OPEC and OSCE). Austrian law long criminalized only espionage *against Austria*, which made it tolerant of everyone else's spying. Its roots go back to the Habsburgs and the 1814–15 Congress of Vienna ([Militär Aktuell interview with historian Thomas Riegler](https://militaeraktuell.at/en/expert-thomas-riegler-on-vienna-the-city-of-espionage/); [The World, 2026](https://theworld.org/stories/2026/03/30/inside-the-trial-shaking-viennas-long-tolerance-for-spies); [HNN](https://www.historynewsnetwork.org/article/128943)). Headline figures such as "7,000 spies" are **[Disputed / weakly sourced]**; a 2018 Austrian official said Brussels now has more ([FMT/AFP](https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/world/2018/06/29/brussels-now-larger-den-of-spies-than-vienna-austrian-official-says)).
**Documented:** the British **Operation SILVER** tapping tunnel (c. 1948–52), the model for Berlin's tunnel ([Encyclopedia.com](https://www.encyclopedia.com/politics/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/berlin-tunnel)). Cultural shorthand: *The Third Man* (1949), with the sewers and the Prater Ferris wheel **[GK]**.
**Playable district pitch:** *Look:* imperial facades, coffeehouses, the Ferris wheel, sewers, a modern UN tower on the edge of the map. *Mood:* polite, ironic, double-dealing. *Era:* 1950s occupation-era zones that "merge" in a later chapter. *Missions:* coffeehouse meets (read the newspaper-signal, choose the right table); conference-hall eavesdropping; sewer chases; a "neutral ground" PvP hub where violence is forbidden and only tradecraft counts.

### London (WWII through the Cold War)
**Why:** home of MI5 and MI6, an imperial and later Commonwealth hub, with huge embassy rows and émigré communities. WWII Allied capital **[GK]**.
**Documented:** Double Cross was run against German agents landed in Britain ([MI5](https://www.mi5.gov.uk/world-war-ii-0)). It was the destination of Gordievsky's exfiltration and home to the Cambridge Five's careers. Landmarks commonly linked to spy history (Bletchley Park outside the city; the MI6 building at Vauxhall Cross) are **[GK]**.
**Playable district pitch:** *Look:* fog, Underground stations, parks, gentlemen's clubs, Thames embankment. *Mood:* understated, class-coded. *Era:* 1940s Double-Cross or 1960s mole-hunt. *Missions:* turn a captured enemy agent and feed false radio traffic (a deception mini-game: keep the story consistent or the enemy notices); a mole hunt (deduction across colleagues); park-bench dead drops.

### Moscow (Cold War)
**Why:** the hardest operating environment for Western officers, under saturation KGB surveillance. That pressure is why the "Moscow Rules" exist ([Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2013/05/17/the-moscow-rules-still-rule/); [Moscow Times](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/archive/russian-and-american-spies-square-off)).
**Documented:**
- **Penkovsky (GRU colonel), 1961–62:** photographed thousands of documents with a Minox camera and used dead drops in public places (reportedly including cigarette packs and candy boxes, and one in a building's radiator or toilet area **[detail varies]**). Courier: British businessman Greville Wynne. In-Moscow contact: Janet Chisholm, wife of an MI6 officer, who met him on walks with her children. Arrested in October 1962 **[Disputed: 22 Oct vs. later dates]** and executed after trial in 1963 ([CIA review essay *The Courier*](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Review-Essay-The-Courier.pdf); [Spartacus Educational](https://spartacus-educational.com/SSpenkovsky.htm)).
- **Operation PIMLICO (1985):** under suspicion, Gordievsky stood at a pre-arranged spot holding a **Safeway carrier bag** as a signal. A British officer walked past with a **Harrods bag eating a Mars bar** **[some say a KitKat; he had been told it might be either]** to acknowledge. Days later he slipped surveillance by going jogging, hid near the Finnish border, and was smuggled across in the boot of a diplomatic car. Pre-planned by MI6's Valerie Pettit since 1978 ([Wikipedia: Oleg Gordievsky](https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleg_Gordievsky); [SPYSCAPE](https://spyscape.com/article/escape-or-die-the-daring-mi6-rescue-of-spymaster-oleg-gordievsky)). When exactly Ames gave Gordievsky's name to the Soviets is **[Disputed]**; one FBI-cited date is 13 June 1985.
- **The Great Seal bug** in the US ambassador's residence, 1945–52.
**Playable district pitch:** *Look:* wide boulevards, metro palaces, snow, identical apartment blocks, the looming security HQ. *Mood:* paranoid. You are always watched. *Era:* 1962 or 1985. *Missions:* the full SDR ("dry-cleaning") run before any contact; a signal-site check (bag, chalk mark, parked car); an exfiltration chain (signal → evade → pickup → border boot-ride, with a "stay calm" stress meter at the checkpoint); bug-sweeping the embassy. This is the "hard mode" world.

### Washington, D.C. and Northern Virginia
**Why:** the main target of Soviet, Cuban and Chinese collection. Home of the CIA (Langley), FBI, NSA (nearby) and embassy row. Today it hosts the **International Spy Museum** **[GK for the geography]**.
**Documented:** Robert Hanssen's dead drop **ELLIS** under a footbridge over Wolftrap Creek in **Foxstone Park, Vienna, *Virginia*** (not Vienna, Austria). He signalled by putting a vertical strip of white medical tape on the park's wooden entrance sign; other sites used thumbtacks and chalk. Each site had a code name. He was arrested there on **18 Feb 2001** ([Wikipedia: Foxstone Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foxstone_Park); [FBI](https://www.fbi.gov/history/famous-cases/robert-hanssen); [FAS-hosted affidavit](https://irp.fas.org/ops/ci/hanssen_affidavit.html)).
**Playable district pitch:** *Look:* marble monuments, leafy suburbs, creek footbridges, strip-mall parking lots. *Mood:* suburban normality hiding betrayal. *Era:* 1985 "Year of the Spy" through 2001. *Missions:* counterintelligence stakeouts (log who visits the signal site, then catch the mole in the act); mailbox chalk marks; the "two sides of the same park" PvP map.

### Istanbul (WWII; also the Cold War)
**Why:** Turkey stayed neutral (an "active neutrality") until February 1945. Istanbul sits on the Bosporus between Europe and Asia, the gateway to the Soviet Black Sea. It was full of émigrés whom every service recruited. German, British, Soviet, Italian, Vichy and Free French agents and Turkish police all worked the same hotels and bars ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/160771041); [Security History Network](https://securityhistorynetwork.com/2024/08/15/the-bosporus-in-the-shadow-of-geopolitics/); [UCLA](https://international.ucla.edu/institute/article/151420)).
**Documented:** the **Pera Palace Hotel** was crowded with agents. A bomb exploded there during the war **[Disputed: 1940 vs. 1941]** ([UCLA](https://international.ucla.edu/institute/article/151420); [Mail & Guardian](https://mg.co.za/article/2010-09-08-landmark-istanbul-hotel-bids-to-revive-glorious-past)). **Operation CICERO** (1943–44): Elyesa Bazna, valet to the British ambassador, photographed secret papers and sold them to the Germans. Note that it was run from **Ankara**, not Istanbul ([CIA, "Footnote to Cicero"](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/Footnote-to-Cicero.pdf)).
**Playable district pitch:** *Look:* minarets, Galata Tower, ferries across the Bosporus, grand hotel lobbies, bazaars. *Mood:* cosmopolitan and slippery, where everyone sells to everyone. *Era:* 1943. *Missions:* the "valet" infiltration (servant cover inside an embassy; photograph documents while the ambassador sleeps); ferry brush passes; bazaar counter-surveillance; information-broker trading (rival teams bid for the same secret).

### Lisbon and Estoril (WWII)
**Why:** neutral Portugal was Europe's last open door to the Atlantic, so refugees, exiled royals and every intelligence service passed through. Portuguese secret police tolerated foreign spying unless it touched internal politics. American reports called Lisbon and Estoril the "spy capital" ([James Bond Dossier](https://www.thejamesbonddossier.com/content/james-bond-where-it-all-began.htm); [Saveur](https://www.saveur.com/from-estoril-with-love/); [British Historical Society of Portugal](https://www.bhsportugal.org/events/report-on-the-ww2-spies-in-lisbon-tour)).
**Documented:** **Dusko Popov** (MI5 double agent TRICYCLE) gambled at the **Estoril Casino**. Ian Fleming, then a naval-intelligence officer, saw him there in 1941, a scene widely linked to *Casino Royale* **[popular account, mostly from Bond writers and Larry Loftis]** ([Larry Loftis](https://larryloftis.com/blog/tale-of-two-casinos); [literary007](https://literary007.com/2017/02/10/ian-fleming-and-the-wwii-espionage-game)). **Juan Pujol García (GARBO)** invented an entire fictional spy network in Britain while living in Lisbon and convinced the Germans it was real ([traveller.com.au](https://www.traveller.com.au/portugal-lisbon-walking-tour-reveals-story-of-spy-who-inspired-james-bond-character-h1ent4)).
**Playable district pitch:** *Look:* tiled facades, trams on steep hills, the river port with flying boats and ships, a glittering seaside casino. *Mood:* glamorous and bittersweet, with refugees waiting for passage. *Era:* 1941. *Missions:* casino card-table meets (a social-stealth mini-game); forge or buy exit visas for refugees (a rescue framing); run a **fictional spy network** like GARBO (a resource-management mode: invent sub-agents and keep their stories consistent); harbour-front handoffs.

### Bern and Geneva (WWII; Geneva after)
**Why:** Switzerland was the only neutral country with a land border with Germany, which made it the best observation post on the Reich ([National Archives](https://text-message.blogs.archives.gov/2012/11/09/allen-dulles-and-no-23-herrengasse-bern-switzerland-1942-1945)). After the war Geneva hosted the League of Nations (earlier), the UN's European office and the big summits **[GK]**.
**Documented:** **Allen Dulles** (OSS) arrived on 9 Nov 1942, minutes before Germany sealed the border. He lived at **Herrengasse 23**, a 14th-century townhouse in Bern's old town above vineyard terraces running down to the Aare River. His door sign read "Special Assistant to the American Minister." He had the streetlight outside turned off, and clandestine visitors came up through the vineyards to the back door ([National Archives](https://text-message.blogs.archives.gov/2012/11/09/allen-dulles-and-no-23-herrengasse-bern-switzerland-1942-1945)).
**Playable district pitch:** *Look:* arcaded medieval streets, a cathedral, vineyards, a lake, trains, banks, conference palaces. *Mood:* quiet, clean, money and secrets. *Era:* 1943–45. *Missions:* receive walk-ins at the back door (judge who is genuine and who is a plant); bank-vault document recovery; summit-week counter-surveillance; a train-border crossing.

### Tangier (and Casablanca)
**Why:** from 1923/24 to 1956 Tangier was an **International Zone** run jointly by several powers (France, Spain, Britain, and later Italy and others **[details vary]**). With political neutrality and commercial freedom, it became a smuggling and spying hub. Spain occupied it on 14 June 1940 ([Tangier American Legation](https://legation.org/1942-2012-operation-torch-70-years-on); [Frommer's](https://www.frommers.com/destinations/tangier)).
**Documented:** the American Legation in Tangier and US "vice-consuls" prepared the ground for **Operation TORCH**, the Allied landings in North Africa in Nov 1942. One OSS courier was tasked with planting a radio beacon near Oran to guide paratroopers ([Legation.org](https://legation.org/1942-2012-operation-torch-70-years-on); [Legation PDF: OSS Diplomatic Courier Run](https://legation.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/OSS-Diplomatic-Courier-Run-Gordon-Browne-1.pdf)). Samples of Nazi-forged British banknotes (Operation BERNHARD) reportedly surfaced at a British bank in Tangier in 1943 **[single source]**. Casablanca's spy reputation is **mostly cinematic** (the 1942 film) **[GK]**.
**Playable district pitch:** *Look:* whitewashed medina, kasbah, port, consulates, the Strait of Gibraltar visible. *Mood:* lawless-romantic, smugglers' town. *Era:* 1942. *Missions:* smuggling-route escorts; plant the landing beacon (an assembly-under-time-pressure puzzle); spot forged currency; a multi-flag diplomatic party.

### Hong Kong (Cold War)
**Why:** a British colony on Communist China's doorstep after 1949. Western services watched the mainland from it, China watched the West, and the KGB was active in the 1970s. A British listening facility monitored Chinese radio traffic **[location: Siu Sai Wan per SCMP]** ([SCMP](https://scmp.com/news/hong-kong/law-crime/article/2129570/ex-cia-agents-arrest-adds-hong-kongs-long-reputation-spying); [Engelsberg Ideas](https://engelsbergideas.com/notebook/hong-kongs-secret-world-of-spies/); [Tom Griffin, MI6 Hong Kong Station](https://intelligencehistory.substack.com/p/mi6-hong-kong-station); [Warwick PhD thesis, Shah 2016](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/81565/)).
**Playable district pitch:** *Look:* neon signs, harbour ferries, junks, dense tenements, hillside villas, a radio-antenna farm. *Mood:* fast and commercial. *Era:* stylized 1960s–70s. *Missions:* signals intercept (tune and triangulate a transmitter); harbour-ferry tails; market brush passes; rooftop chases. **Sensitivity:** current Hong Kong and mainland politics are live issues, so keep it firmly historical and fictionalized, or substitute a fictional "Harbour City."

### Havana (Cold War)
**Why:** 90 miles from Florida. After 1959 it was a Soviet ally, and the CIA's Havana station was shut. The agency then ran Cuba operations from Miami (JMWAVE, at one time the largest CIA station in the world) ([CIA review: *Covert City: Miami*](https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/static/5-Review-Covert-City-Miami.pdf)). During the 1962 October Crisis the **Hotel Nacional** had trenches and anti-aircraft guns on its grounds; its tunnels now hold a small museum ([Atlas Obscura](https://atlasobscura.com/places/hotel-nacional-de-cuba); [Tico Times](https://ticotimes.net/travel/hotel-nacional-de-cuba-a-history-of-mobsters-missiles-and-movie-stars)). Graham Greene's *Our Man in Havana* (1958) satirizes spying with a vacuum-cleaner salesman who invents agents and passes off drawings of vacuum parts as weapons plans **[GK]**.
**Playable district pitch:** *Look:* pastel colonial streets, vintage cars, the Malecón seawall, a grand hotel with a cannon battery. *Mood:* bright on the surface, tense underneath. *Era:* 1962. *Missions:* photograph "agricultural equipment" at the port (recon); a Greene-style comic mission where an informant's intel turns out to be vacuum-cleaner diagrams; numbers-station decoding. **Sensitivity:** Cuba–US relations are still live; keep it historical.

### Beirut (1950s–early 1970s)
**Why:** the free-wheeling banking and press capital of the Arab world, the "Paris of the Middle East," and a crossroads for every service in the region **[GK]**. The **St. Georges Hotel bar** was "spook central" for spies and journalists. Kim Philby lived there as a journalist, under suspicion. In January 1963, shortly after MI6's Nicholas Elliott confronted him, he vanished, reportedly aboard a Soviet freighter, and resurfaced in Moscow ([Carnegie Endowment photo essay](https://carnegieendowment.org/middle-east/diwan/2019/10/photo-essay-what-remains-of-kim-philbys-beirut); [The National](https://thenational-the-national-prod.cdn.arcpublishing.com/arts-culture/books/2021/10/07/kim-philbys-beirut-the-missing-years-in-the-life-of-the-notorious-british-spy)).
**Playable district pitch:** *Look:* seafront corniche, a hotel bar, yachts, a souk, mountain villas. *Mood:* sun-soaked decadence. *Era:* 1962–63 only. **Sensitivity:** later Beirut is the 1975–90 civil war; keep the era strictly pre-1975.

### Others to consider later
Cairo and Alexandria (WWII); Helsinki (Finland as a neutral neighbour of the USSR, and the end of the Gordievsky route); Mexico City (a Cold War hub for Soviet walk-ins) **[all GK]**.

## 3. Tradecraft, explained for mechanics

Definitions follow the International Spy Museum's glossary ([Language of Espionage](https://spymuseum.org/education-programs/spy-resources/language-of-espionage/)) and the CIA's ([Spy Glossary](https://www.cia.gov/resources/spy-glossary/)) unless noted. Each entry ends with a **one-line mechanic**.

| Tradecraft | What it is (public sources) | Real example | One-line game mechanic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dead drop** (dead letter box) | A hidden location where one party leaves material for another to collect, so the two never meet. Containers are mundane: hollow spikes, fake rocks, crevices. Typical contents are film, money, instructions, cipher pads ([Crypto Museum: dead drops](https://www.cryptomuseum.com/covert/deaddrop/)). | Hanssen's "ELLIS" under a creek footbridge. | Hide an item in any world prop; another player can only find it from your encoded description, and anyone watching the spot can steal or photograph it. |
| **Signal site** | A public mark that tells the other side "drop loaded", "drop emptied" or "meet tonight": tape, chalk, a thumbtack, a parked car, an object in a window. | Hanssen's vertical tape on the Foxstone Park sign; Gordievsky's Safeway bag. | Place or read a small mark (chalk, tape, flowerpot); the colour/position grammar is agreed per mission, and a wrong mark aborts the drop. |
| **Brush pass** (flash pass) | A split-second handoff between two people passing in a crowd, with no conversation. | Common in Moscow Rules accounts ([SPYSCAPE glossary](https://spyscape.com/article/spy-glossary)). | A timing mini-game: both players path through a crowd and must cross within a half-second window while out of the watcher's line of sight. |
| **Surveillance detection route (SDR), "dry cleaning"** | A long, pre-planned route that forces followers to show themselves (repeated sightings over time and distance) before an operational act. Often hours long, mixing car, public transit and walking ([Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2013/05/17/the-moscow-rules-still-rule/)). | CIA Moscow station practice. | Before any objective, run a route with "choke points"; the game flags faces/cars you've seen twice, and you only proceed "black" (clean) when the heat meter is zero. |
| **Cover and legend** | *Cover* is your purported job or purpose. It must fit your background and your presence in the place. *Legend* is your claimed life story, supported by documents and memorized details. | Dulles as "Special Assistant to the American Minister"; Eichmann team's false passports. | Pick a cover (diplomat, journalist, tourist, businessman); it unlocks some areas and conversations and blocks others, and NPC questions test your memorized legend. |
| **Cut-out** | A person or mechanism that sits between two members of a network so neither can identify the other. | Greville Wynne as Penkovsky's courier. | Route messages through an NPC courier: slower and costs favour, but if one node is caught the trail stops there. |
| **One-time pad (OTP)** | A random key used once; used properly it is mathematically unbreakable. Reusing a pad is the classic fatal error ([Spy Museum glossary](https://spymuseum.org/education-programs/spy-resources/language-of-espionage/); [Wikipedia: Numbers station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numbers_station)). | Used with numbers stations; Soviet pad reuse enabled the US VENONA decrypts **[GK]**. | A decoding puzzle with a pad page from your inventory; reusing a pad page lets the opposing team decrypt your traffic. |
| **Numbers station** | Shortwave broadcasts of spoken digit groups, peaking in the Cold War, presumed to carry OTP-encrypted orders to agents in the field. The "Lincolnshire Poacher" opened with bars of an English folk tune and was traced to Cyprus; its link to SIS is **attributed, not officially confirmed** ([Wikipedia: Lincolnshire Poacher](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincolnshire_Poacher_(numbers_station))). | Cuba's DI reportedly tasked Ana Montes this way **[GK]**. | A world radio plays a daily broadcast; tune in at the right hour, copy the digits, decode with your pad → that's your next secret objective. |
| **Concealment device** | Ordinary objects hollowed out to hide film, cash or codes: coins, batteries, books, cigarette packs, candy boxes. | Penkovsky's cigarette packs and candy boxes. | Every container prop can be "rigged"; searches by guards take time, so better concealment buys seconds. |
| **Safe house** | A residence controlled by the service, used for meetings, debriefs or hiding people. | The Eichmann-team safe house in Buenos Aires. | A player base: limited slots, "burned" if a tail follows you inside, and needs to be relocated. |
| **Walk-in** | Someone who approaches an embassy or official installation unprompted to volunteer secrets or defect ([Spy Museum glossary](https://spymuseum.org/education-programs/spy-resources/language-of-espionage/)). | Visitors at Dulles's back door. | Random NPC volunteers arrive; you must vet them (genuine, plant, or crank?) using clues before trusting their tips. |
| **Exfiltration** | A covert operation to bring a defector, agent or their family to safety ([Spy Museum glossary](https://spymuseum.org/education-programs/spy-resources/language-of-espionage/)). | PIMLICO: signal → shake surveillance → rendezvous → car boot over the border. | A multi-stage escort event with fixed signals and a fallback date, plus a "calm" meter while hidden at the checkpoint. |
| **Bugging / technical surveillance** | Hidden microphones and taps on phones and cables. | The Great Seal bug; the Berlin Tunnel tapping. | Plant bugs in rooms to hear NPC/enemy-team intel; the other team can sweep for them with a detector. |
| **Agent recruitment cycle** | Spot → assess → develop → recruit → handle → terminate. Western teaching often summarizes motives as **MICE** (Money, Ideology, Coercion/Compromise, Ego) **[GK; the acronym is widely used but not on the Spy Museum glossary page]**. | Montes (ideology); Ames and Hanssen (money); Penkovsky (ideology plus ego **[GK]**). | A relationship mini-game with NPC "assets": learn their needs over several meetings, then pitch; **only trust-based motives (belief, recognition, help, fair payment) are used in-game, never coercion or seduction** (section 5). |
| **Mole / double agent** | Someone who joins one service while working for another; the term was popularized by le Carré ([Spy Museum glossary](https://spymuseum.org/education-programs/spy-resources/language-of-espionage/)). | Blake, Philby, Ames, Hanssen, Koecher. | Social-deduction layer: one player per match is secretly working for the other side, and the team has to work out who. |
| **Deception / feeding a false network** | Turned agents send controlled false information back to their original service. | Double Cross, GARBO. | Keep a fictional network's reports consistent over time; contradictions raise enemy suspicion. |
| **Prisoner or spy swap** | Captured agents traded at a neutral point, simultaneously, brokered by intermediaries. | The Glienicke Bridge in 1962 and 1986; Wolfgang Vogel. | Captured players aren't eliminated; they're "held" until a negotiated swap event on a bridge map. |

## 5. Sensitivities: depicting real nations and agencies in a family home

The game should be playable in a family home. Recommendation:

1. **Fictional agencies, real-feeling cities.** Playable factions get **invented names and emblems**, not CIA, KGB, Mossad and so on. They can be *inspired by* a real service's style (e.g. "the gadget-rich service," "the patient long-game service"), but each faction must have heroes and flaws, so no real nation is "the bad guys." Cities can stay real (Berlin, Lisbon). Real landmarks can be **lightly renamed or stylized** (e.g. "the Bridge of Exchanges"), which also avoids implying endorsement.
2. **No real living (or recently living) people as characters.** No Sharansky, no Gordievsky-as-character, no real officers. Historical events can inspire *fictional* missions ("a defector's signal with a shopping bag") without naming anyone. A museum-style "Real History" codex can name real people respectfully, in the past tense, with sources.
3. **Stylized past, not the present.** Set worlds in a stylized 1940s–1980s. Keep away from current conflicts (Russia–Ukraine, Israel–Gaza, China–Taiwan and Hong Kong, Cuba–US), and from Beirut after 1975.
4. **Remove these from mechanics entirely:**
   - Seduction or "honey trap" and "Romeo" operations.
   - Coercion or blackmail as a recruitment lever.
   - Interrogation and torture.
   - Assassination and poison gadgets.
   - Executions.
   - Recruitment becomes a **trust-and-persuasion** game: help the asset, earn their belief, pay fairly. Capture leads to a **swap**, not death. "Takedowns" are non-lethal (tagging, photographing, unmasking).
5. **Atrocity-linked history stays in the codex, never in play.** The Eichmann capture is tied to the Holocaust. Write it up respectfully in the history codex; **never** make it a playable or gamified mission. Likewise, the Stasi HQ at Normannenstraße is a **memorial** to people who were surveilled and persecuted. Present the surveillance state as the *system you evade and outwit*, never as a fun faction to join, and never re-enact real victims' stories.
6. **Respectful national texture.** Real food, music, architecture and languages, researched properly. No caricature accents or ethnic villains.
7. **Age-rating sanity check.** Keeping combat non-lethal and removing the themes above should keep the game in the youngest practical rating band **[not legal advice; check ESRB/PEGI criteria at build time]**.

**Bottom line:** *real places and real techniques; fictional agencies, people and plots; no killing, seduction or coercion; history honoured in a codex.*

## 6. Shortlist: worlds to build, in order

Criteria: density of documented, family-safe history; how directly that history maps onto the core mechanics (drops, signals, SDRs, swaps, exfiltration); visual distinctness from the other worlds; and whether it has a natural two-team multiplayer split.

1. **Berlin, 1961–63: "Bridge & Tunnel."** **(Vertical slice; build first.)** A city literally split into two playable halves, which makes it the cleanest map for a two-team game. It packs the most canonical, kid-safe set pieces into one district: the bridge swap at dawn (the Glienicke Bridge, 1962), the wiretap tunnel (Operation GOLD) with its built-in twist that the other side knew all along (Blake), sector-border crossings, U-Bahn tails and rubble-lot dead drops. The bridge swap also *solves* "what happens when you're caught" without violence. Grey, rainy and searchlit, it sets the game's visual and tonal benchmark. **Why first:** every core system (cover, SDR, signals, dead drops, bugging, capture-and-swap, two-team PvP) gets exercised in one compact map. Its WWII-free 1960s setting also avoids the Nazi-era context that Lisbon and Istanbul carry.
2. **Lisbon and Estoril, 1941: "The Last Open Door."** The glamour world: casino social-stealth, trams and harbours, and a **GARBO-style "fictional network" mode** where you run invented agents and keep the lies consistent. Its rescue framing (exit visas for refugees) gives it heart. Treat the WWII context with care: the adversary is a fictional regime, with no swastikas.
3. **Moscow, 1985: "Moscow Rules."** The hard-mode world. Surveillance is everywhere, so every action needs an SDR first. Its climax is a PIMLICO-style exfiltration (shopping-bag signal, a jog to shake the tail, a border crossing in a car boot) with a "stay calm" meter. It teaches the patience the rest of the game rewards.
4. **Istanbul, 1943: "Crossroads."** The information-market world. Every faction buys and sells the same secrets in grand hotels, bazaars and Bosporus ferries. Signature job: the CICERO-style "servant cover" infiltration (set in a fictional embassy). Visually the warmest and most colourful contrast to Berlin and Moscow.
5. **Vienna, 1950s: "The Neutral Ground."** Coffeehouses, an imperial city, sewers and a Ferris wheel. Doubles as the **social hub**: a neutral zone where no "takedowns" are allowed, so only tradecraft, trades and negotiated swaps happen. Hosts cross-faction events.
6. **Havana, 1962: "Ninety Miles."** Bright pastel colour, vintage cars, the Malecón and the hotel-with-a-gun-battery. Recon and numbers-station decoding, plus a Greene-style comic mission (the "vacuum-cleaner plans"). Keep it strictly historical and fictionalized, given live US–Cuba sensitivities.

*Bench (later):* Bern/Geneva (walk-ins at the back door, banks, trains), Tangier (smugglers and planting the landing beacon), Washington's suburbs (mole hunt at a creek footbridge), Hong Kong (signals intercept; or a fictional "Harbour City"), Beirut 1962 (hotel-bar intrigue).
